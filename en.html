Marketing and sales data automation for a real estate developer

ETL pipelines on Azure Databricks, automated exploratory analysis and a Power BI report to decide every week where to invest in advertising.

The challenge

The sales team ran campaigns on Meta and Google, booked appointments in Calendly and Google Sheets, and tracked reservations, sales and cancellations in its web platform. Each source lived on its own, and the weekly report for leadership was put together by hand.

The goal was to connect ad spend with what happens next, to measure the real cost of each appointment, reservation and sale, and to adjust spend week by week.

My responsibilities

  • Design and orchestration of the Databricks jobs.
  • API connections and OAuth 2.0 authentication for Calendly.
  • Data cleaning and governance rules.
  • Exploratory analysis and KPI definition.
  • Data model, DAX measures and Power BI report design.
  • Weekly presentation of results to leadership.

Key measures in the model

Cost per appointment = Total ad spend / Appointments
Cost per reservation = Total ad spend / Reservations
% Conversion = (Reservations / Appointments) × 100
% ROI = (Sales − Spend) / Spend × 100

The model has 8 tables: appointments, costs and spend by channel, web transactions, KPIs and calendar. The report covers sales and cancellations from 2022 to 2025.

Results

  • Over 60% less time spent on manual analysis: the report is no longer assembled by hand.
  • Standardized reports so leadership can decide ad spend with data every week.
  • Identified the time slots when ads perform best.
  • Automatic WhatsApp messages to records with a “No response” status, without the sales agent having to look for them.
  • On every run: outliers with box plots, correlations between clicks, cost per click and conversions, and a ranking of the 5 best and worst ads.

Data pipeline, profitability analysis and machine learning for a social and corporate events company in Saltillo, with real data from 2021 to 2026.

The challenge

The company provides DJ, sound and lighting for quinceañeras, weddings, holiday parties, birthdays and corporate events. The whole operation lived in one Google Sheets calendar per season, each with different columns and formats.

Decisions on how much to spend on ads, in which months and which packages to push were made on gut feeling. The goal was to find what drives bookings, prioritize high-ticket events and anticipate the next 12 months.

Business questions

  • Which event type makes the most money: the best seller or the one with the highest margin?
  • In which months do sales happen, and how far in advance does each type of client book?
  • How much does each booking cost in ads, and is it worth spending more?
  • How many events and how much revenue should we expect over the next 12 months?
  • Which months fail to cover fixed costs?

A pipeline that rebuilds with one command

run_pipeline.py runs 11 steps in order with nbconvert, re-reads the calendars from Google Sheets and stops at any step that fails. It can resume from any step or run a single one.

StepComponentOutput
1 to 6limpieza_<temporada>.ipynbOne clean calendar per season: 2020, 2021‑22, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026
7 and 8ads_2025, ads_2026Monthly Meta Ads spend
9build_historico.pyConsolidated history in CSV and Parquet, with duplicates resolved
10 and 1101_historico, 02_analisis_visualMetrics, charts and a monthly table for Power BI
Separate03_forecasting_ml.ipynbModels, backtest, 12-month forecast and executive summary

Usage

# Run all 11 steps
python run_pipeline.py

# Resume from step 7
python run_pipeline.py --desde 7

# Run only the consolidation
python run_pipeline.py --solo 9

Cleaning rules

  • Each sheet read directly with pandas, no copy-pasting.
  • Common schema: renamed columns and phantom rows removed.
  • Mixed date formats. I found a bug that silently dropped 2026 dates that included a time.
  • Amounts with text, symbols and commas converted to numbers.
  • Repeat clients across seasons flagged, and split contracts merged into a single event.
  • 31 hand-typed event-type labels grouped into 13 types.

When sales happen, and how far ahead

December sells 2.2 times an average month, June 2.0 and August 1.8. January, February and April sit between 0.2 and 0.3. By contract date, May, September and November have the most bookings.

Lead time varies a lot by event type, so each campaign has to go out with its own lead time.

In 2026, monthly spend rose 114%, but bookings per month rose only 30%. 2025 includes 11 months with ad spend.

A negative binomial model estimated an elasticity of 0.38 (95% CI: 0.09 to 0.66): 10% more spend brings about 3.8% more bookings. The next improvement isn’t spending more, but choosing when and on which event type to invest.

With 20 months of data the result is indicative, and the relationship may run the other way: more is spent in high season.

Six models and one rule: beat the baseline

ModelIdeaHorizon
M1 Seasonal naiveSame month last year times annual growthBaseline
M2 Holt-WintersDamped trend and 12-month seasonality1 to 12 months
M3 SARIMAAIC search on the log-transformed series1 to 12 months
M4 Booking curveWhat’s already booked divided by the share usually booked at that lead time1 to 3 months
M5 Gradient Boosting and Random ForestLags, month, trend and bookings1 to 12 months
M6 AdsNegative binomial: bookings by log(spend) and seasonalityScenarios

Three tests without peeking at the future: fixed origin (train through October 2025 and forecast the rest), rolling origin (1, 3 and 6 months ahead from each month of 2025 and 2026) and booking curve (simulate being 60, 30 and 15 days before each month). The metric is WAPE; I didn’t use MAPE because some months have zero events.

The tree-based models did worse than the baseline: with about 53 months of training data they learn noise and can’t project a growing series. SARIMA won for 4 to 12 months, and the booking curve plus model won for 1 to 3 months.

The next 12 months

The forecast for November 2026 to October 2027 expects 265 events (80% range: 144 to 507), MXN 1.97 million in gross revenue and MXN 587k in net profit, about MXN 48,900 per month.

It assumes the business keeps growing 49% in events versus the last 12 months. If growth slows, the lower band is the reference.

What the company got

  • A single reliable source with 502 events from 6 seasons, rebuilt with one command.
  • Knowing what makes money: quinceañeras bring volume; smaller and corporate events bring margin.
  • An advertising calendar based on each event type’s seasonality and lead time.
  • Evidence to avoid overspending on ads.
  • A 12-month forecast with at-risk months and booking alerts.

Limitations

  • Net profit only has 22 months of history and is computed as revenue times margin.
  • The ads model uses 20 months of data and is indicative.
  • Package classification is heuristic, based on keywords in the text.
  • Business changes, such as a minimum price, are evaluated as scenarios rather than inside the model.
